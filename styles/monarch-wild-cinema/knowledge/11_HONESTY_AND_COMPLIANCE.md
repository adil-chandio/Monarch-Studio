# 11 · Honesty and compliance

## The two findings that shaped this style

**Finding 1 — the exposure is the footage.** The channel's winning video
(955,681 views) is a compilation of clips it does not own. YouTube's 2025
"inauthentic content" policy evaluates the **whole channel**, permission does
not exempt it, and "no copyright infringement intended" plus a Section 107 line
is not a defence. For a channel whose entire library is reposted material, this
is not a risk — it is a standing exposure.

**Finding 2 — the audience rewards the idea, not the footage.** The channel's
worst-performing upload was AI narration over a static ranking: **16 views,
0 likes**. The channel already contains the proof of what works and what does
not.

Both findings point the same way: **generate everything, own everything, and
put the value in the explanation rather than in the clip.** A production with
generated plates, synthesised score, generated typography and its own script
has no third-party material in it at all.

## The honest-hook doctrine

The forensic audit's cleanest lesson is a comparison, and it is worth stating
as a rule:

> **A hook may promise the truth dramatically. It may not promise something
> the video does not deliver.**

The winning video's hook was strong *and* accurate. The temptation with a
footage-free production is to compensate with an overstated title — "this bear
DESTROYED him" over a video about bear deterrence. That is the exact trade that
made the previous format die: the thumbnail over-promised, the video
under-delivered, and the retention curve said so.

### Applied rules

1. **Every number spoken or drawn has a source**, named in the narration, in
   the on-screen `source_stamp`, or in the description's SOURCES block.
2. **No comparison the sources do not support** — see chapter 07 and episode
   01's refused spray-vs-firearms chart. Saying "these are two different
   samples and the studies do not rank them" is *better television* than a
   fake bar chart, because it is the kind of thing a viewer repeats to someone
   else.
3. **No medical, legal or safety advice beyond the cited sources.** Bear
   safety guidance is quoted from NPS and the relevant literature, attributed.
4. **Distinguish anecdote from data** on screen. An incident is an incident;
   a measured rate is a rate.
5. **State the uncertainty in the same breath as the claim.** "About nine in
   ten", not "90 % of the time".

## The synthetic-content disclosure

When the cut contains generated plates or synthesised narration — which is
every production of this style — the upload must answer YouTube's
**"Altered or synthetic content"** question with **YES**.

Why this is not a penalty:

- The label is *accurate*. The visuals and the voice are synthetic.
- It is **transparency about the medium**, not about the intent. The animation
  is a rendering technique, in the same category as a documentary
  reconstruction.
- The alternative — an undisclosed synthetic production — is a policy strike
  waiting for a reviewer to find it, on a channel that already carries
  reposted-content risk.

A one-line narration-synthetic disclosure in the description is optional but
recommended, and costs nothing when the video's honesty is already its selling
point.

## What this style refuses outright

| refused | why |
|---|---|
| third-party footage | the channel's single largest policy exposure |
| licensed or unlicensed music | Content ID; the score is synthesised instead |
| stock overlay packs | compliance, and they cannot be parameterised |
| unsupported comparisons | it converts a knowledge channel into a misinformation channel |
| a claim without a source | the audience can check; they should be able to |
| a depth prior that inverts on our own grade | it is a defect, not a preference (T01) |
| a caption band or karaoke pill | explicitly rejected by the channel owner in WF-05 |
| an approval field filled from intention | an empty field is more honest and more useful |

## The disclosure checklist, every upload

- [ ] Altered or synthetic content → **YES**
- [ ] Every on-screen number appears in the description's SOURCES block
- [ ] No supers/comparison implies a ranking the sources refuse
- [ ] Any incident shown is labelled as an incident, not a statistic
- [ ] Sources named in the video itself, not only in the description
