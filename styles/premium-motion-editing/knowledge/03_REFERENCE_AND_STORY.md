# 03 · Reference inspection, story and accurate adaptation

## Inspect first, not after rendering

Recover the supplied reference and any prior outputs before generating replacements. Read metadata, save source IDs/hashes, inspect the complete sequence with contact sheets, then inspect dense frames around transitions, moving labels and tag locations. A file name or a prior agent's summary is not visual evidence.

For this case the recovered reference was 720×1282 at 30 fps; the 720×1280 working stage removed one pixel from top and bottom. The original social ending was excluded after source time 51.64s. Full duration and container/audio duration can differ slightly; do not invent a frame count from an approximate player display.

Build an inspection ledger: source time, subject, object state, camera behavior, typography region, tag corner, transition, and likely edit boundary. Sparse samples can tell you the style; they cannot certify the exact cut. The watch-to-flow and flow-to-car boundaries required denser inspection.

## Argument spine of the case

The supplied story moves from an Einstein/gravity anecdote to a musical change of mental mode, to a claim about flow and AI shortcuts; it then uses cars versus running as an analogy for chosen effort, extends that analogy to the brain, introduces “creative inflation,” contrasts efficiency and talent, and closes on people paying to carry heavy sandbags and what that suggests about art.

That structure is an argument, not a list of decorative nouns. Each visual performs a job: establish pressure, change state, contrast convenience with practice, show divergence, then leave an unresolved consequence. The shot map in the case project records all fourteen observed mechanisms.

## Translation method

Preserve meaning, causality, emphasis and tone in easy Urdu-English. Keep useful terms such as flow state, AI shortcuts, efficiency and creative inflation where they are clearer than a forced substitute. Break spoken lines at meaningful clauses. Roman Urdu on-screen wording should be short enough to read while watching motion.

Do not change the argument merely to fit an English source duration. Do not shrink a whole translated paragraph to microscopic text. Do not equate a caption transcript with certified word-by-word speech recognition. The selected audio files remain the spoken authority; the recorded Roman Urdu phrases are the on-screen script.

## Fact and metaphor boundaries

The Einstein/Mozart anecdote was adapted from the supplied reference, not independently verified history. The opening “Ek mashhoor kahani” preserves that boundary. Claims about shortcuts, creativity and talent are the reference's rhetoric; a visual metaphor is not evidence of a scientific causal law.

For a new topic, make a claim ledger: assertion, source or user-provided premise, confidence, and wording. Mark uncertain anecdotes as stories. Do not fabricate dates, quotes, studies, footage provenance or scientific certainty. Avoid using body shape or disability imagery as proof of laziness, worth or cognitive ability.

## Reuse scope

If the task is a reference adaptation, preserve the reference's essential motion grammar and argument. If the task is a new production, create a new topic-specific argument and matching motion assets. An Einstein replay is not a completed new video about a different topic simply because its title changed.
