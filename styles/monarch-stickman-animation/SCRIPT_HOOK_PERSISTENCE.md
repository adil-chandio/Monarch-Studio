## Specificity density
Hard facts delivered: two hours · forty minutes · forty-two degrees · four
million sweat glands · ten times a chimpanzee · panting vs gait-locked gallop ·
Namib · thirty-nine degrees · forty-four degrees. **Nine verifiable specifics in
fifty seconds** — roughly one every 5.5 s. That is the retention engine, not the
animation.

## Sources
- **Eccrine sweat glands, 2–4 million, ~10x chimpanzee density** — endurance
  pursuit literature; Bramble & Lieberman, *Nature* 432 (2004) "Endurance
  running and the evolution of Homo".
- **Gait-locked breathing** — Bramble & Carrier (1983): quadrupeds are fixed at
  a 1:1 stride-to-breath ratio at trot and gallop; humans use 2:1, and also
  4:1, 3:1, 5:2, 3:2. Panting cannot continue through a gallop.
- **Gallop limit 10–15 minutes** before the animal must drop to a trot —
  Bramble & Lieberman (2004).
- **Kalahari persistence hunts: 2–5 hours, 25–35 km, 40–42 °C** — Liebenberg,
  *Current Anthropology* (2006), 8 measured hunts, mean 2.3 h.
- **Namib oryx pursuit: hunter stayed below 39 °C while the oryx reached
  44 °C** — field thermoregulation study, ambient 36–41 °C.
- **Endurance running adaptations appear ~2 million years ago** in genus Homo:
  nuchal ligament (absent in apes), long Achilles tendon, plantar arch
  returning ~17 % of stance energy.

## Why this topic
It is contested in the literature (some researchers dispute how central
persistence hunting was to human evolution). The script therefore states only
the physiology — which is not disputed — and attributes the field observations
to the researchers who made them. Never assert the contested evolutionary claim
as settled fact.
