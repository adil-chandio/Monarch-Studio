A charging grizzly can close 40 metres in three seconds. Your top speed is
15 mph. His is 35. And in almost every real bear encounter caught on camera,
the person does the same thing — they run. That instinct is what turns a
warning into a hunt.

Most bear charges are not attacks. They are tests. This is what the bear is
actually reading, why running fails, and the four different answers that
depend on which bear is in front of you.

📚 SOURCES
• Smith, Herrero et al. — "Efficacy of Bear Deterrent Spray in Alaska",
  Journal of Wildlife Management: bear spray deterred aggressive bears in
  ~92% of cases; 98% of people carrying spray were uninjured.
• Smith, Herrero et al. — "Efficacy of Firearms for Bear Deterrence in
  Alaska": firearms succeeded in ~76% of cases.
• National Park Service bear safety guidance: play dead for a defensive
  grizzly attack; fight back against a black bear.
• Top speed figures: National Wildlife Federation / Park Service records
  (grizzly sprint ~35 mph; sustained 25–28 mph over up to 2 miles);
  Usain Bolt peak 27.8 mph, Berlin 2009.

come out of them. Every episode: what happened, what the animal was
actually doing, and what to do instead.

and sound. No third-party footage was used.
