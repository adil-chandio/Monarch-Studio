## VO SCRIPT (final)

A charging grizzly can close forty metres in about three seconds.
Your top speed is fifteen miles an hour. His is thirty-five.
And yet, in almost every real bear encounter caught on camera, the person does the same thing.
They run.
That instinct is exactly what turns a warning into a hunt.

Here is what most people are never told.
Most bear charges are not attacks. They are tests.
A bear that intends to kill does not announce it. It comes quietly, from behind, at night.
A bear that charges you head-on — huffing, popping its jaw, slapping the ground — that bear is reading you.
It wants to know one thing. Are you a threat, or are you prey.
Running answers that question for it.

Bears are built to chase.
Their eyes lock onto movement. Anything that flees is food.
So when you run, you do not escape the encounter. You start a different one.
And you lose it.
Usain Bolt reached twenty-seven miles an hour for a few seconds.
A grizzly holds thirty-five across broken ground, uphill, for a mile.
There is no version of this where you win.

And this is the part that gets people killed.
The right move depends entirely on who is in front of you.

A black bear that charges — fight back.
Make yourself huge. Shout. Throw things. Swing. Black bears back down.
A grizzly that charges — do not run. Do not fight.
Speak low, back away slowly. If it makes contact, play dead. Face down, hands behind your neck, legs apart.
A polar bear — there is no playing dead.
Nothing works on a polar bear except deterrents. That is why the Arctic runs on spray and rifles.
And a sloth bear — the one bear that hunts people on purpose — the only answer is to fight, and to fight loud.

Now, the number that matters.
Researchers reviewed a century of bear encounters.
Firearms stopped a charging bear in about two thirds of cases.
Bear spray stopped it in more than nine out of ten.
The spray works because it does not need accuracy, and it does not need you to be calm.
It puts a wall of pain between you and an animal that can end you with one swipe.
If you are going into bear country, that can is not optional.

So remember three things.
Noise, so you are never a surprise.
Distance, so you are never a decision.
And if it charges — stand still, speak low, and let the bear work out that you were never worth it.

Bears do not want to fight you.
They want you to leave.
Give them that, and almost every one of these encounters ends the same way.
With a story, and a pulse.
