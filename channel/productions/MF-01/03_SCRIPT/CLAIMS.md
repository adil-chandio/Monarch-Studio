## Claims deliberately NOT made

Two things the topic invites that were left out because the evidence is genuinely contested:

1. **"Bear spray is better than a firearm."** The Alaska studies show higher deterrence rates for spray, but critics (including the linked Craig Medred analysis) raise serious selection-bias objections — the firearms sample skews to pre-1990 cases and excludes successful defence-of-life killings. The video states the two numbers side by side and lets the viewer conclude. **No superiority claim.**

2. **"Bears can't climb trees / running downhill works / etc."** Popular myth material with no clean sourcing. Not included.

---

## Scoring boundary stated in the video

The video says: *"Researchers reviewed a century of bear encounters in Alaska."* — this is accurate: Smith & Herrero's firearm analysis covers attacks from the late 1800s to 2009 (269 attacks, all three species). The 2008 spray study covers 1985–2006 (72 cases).

If a commenter objects that the samples differ, the correct response is the "deliberately not made" section above — the studies are not directly comparable, which is why no superiority claim is made.
